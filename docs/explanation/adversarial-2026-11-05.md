# Adversarial premortem — 2026-11-05

Applied to `docs/consolidation.md`. Same battery as the earlier pass (premortem, steelman, assumptions, second order, red team, verdict, adversary, blind spots, reconstruction). The plan under test is the consolidated engine, not a subject.

The recast that removed every sample subject landed before this pass. This pass is the correction to that recast.

## 1. Premortem

It is six months from now. The consolidated engine failed completely.

How it died, ranked:

1. **Menu, no unit.** Seven strategies were written as peers. None of them has a public URL or a tracked submission. The subject ban made a real job feel like a policy violation, so the template is still the only job.
2. **The only runnable tree was deleted.** A fixture could render a picture. The replacement cannot emit a frame. Domain-agnostic became file-agnostic.
3. **The check is the product.** `just check` is green. There is nothing to watch or hear.
4. **Two repos.** A local commit and a GitHub repo that this environment cannot read. They never became the same history.
5. **Review ate the proof window.** Consolidation and this premortem used the days the kill test had assigned to one submission or one public piece.
6. **Every first step is outside the repo, and none of them happened.** Funded needs a brief. Owned needs a master on disk. Generated needs a cap and a render. The repo cannot do those, and nobody else did.
7. **Extensions snuck in as strategy.** Audio-as-product, procedural formats, and a SKU were specified before one ordinary piece existed.
8. **Tool invoices with no measure.** Rented generators and clippers billed a month. The log is empty.
9. **Identity never got a name.** `distribution_identity` is null. Posts, if any, landed on whatever account was already logged in.
10. **Hour cap missed.** Content work spilled past the cap and crowded out the non-content obligation that keeps the lights on.

Earliest sign: day 14, still no tracked submission and no public URL.

Hidden assumption: a subject-free contract is more useful than one shipped piece whose subject lives only in the job folder.

## 2. Steelman

The strongest case against this plan: do not build an engine. The playbooks already say rent a clipper or a generator, post, and write down the URL. A repository of strategies is how a month passes with no file. Salary, or whatever already pays the bills, has clearer unit economics than a new identity posting one piece a day against people who post all day.

Strongest point: a single operator loses a volume game, and a schema does not change that.

Counter: the plan is not the volume game. It is one proof inside a hard hour cap, then a kill. The steelman wins if that cap is not held.

## 5. Red team

- The brief changes after the work is done. Unpaid.
- Only one person can post or submit. A travel day misses the window.
- The local tree and the GitHub tree diverge, and the next session edits the wrong one.
- Too many channel writeups. The operator mutes them.
- Synthetic media goes out undeclared.
- A funded file is posted on the identity meant for work you own.
- "Ship" pressure lowers the veto. The identity gets a spam reputation.

## 6. Verdict

**YES-IF** by day 14 there is one proof: a funded submission that is tracking, **or** one public URL of a piece from a master you hold or a generation you paid for.

**Why the shape is still right:** three ways in, one identity each, rent the file, own the log, human publishes, no unlicensed source. That is what the playbooks actually shared.

**The hole:** time, and a tree that cannot make media. The seven-way menu and the green check do not move either.

**Would flip the verdict:** a new identity cannot get a funded brief accepted, and three public pieces each sit under 200 views with a real hook. Then stop. Do not add a format.

**Confidence:** medium. It hangs on the calendar, not on the document.

## 8. Blind spots

1. Payout thresholds and currency conversion delay the moment money is felt.
2. Disclosure rules differ by platform inside one brief.
3. A bad room ruins the audio of an otherwise fine piece.
4. The job folder is on a machine the operator does not open.
5. The identity's name trips a spam heuristic.
6. Budgets move with the season. A rate in one month is not the next month.
7. Sessions that only write docs raise the bill and not the measure.
8. Two copies of the same script. The wrong one gets recorded.
9. One logged-in profile posts a funded cut onto an identity you meant to keep.
10. A reminder fires in the wrong hour and gets ignored.
